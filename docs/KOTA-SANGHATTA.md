## Kota Chakra (printed pp.226-240, verses 1-95; the structure at pp.230-231)
- Twenty-eight stars, Abhijit included, in three parts of a fort: outer (Bahya) 12 - Krittika, Pushya, Ashlesha, Magha, Swati, Vishakha, Anuradha, Abhijit, Shravana, Dhanishta, Ashwini, Bharani (v.20); wall (Prakara) 8 - Rohini, Punarvasu, Purvaphalguni, Chitra, Jyeshtha, Uttarashadha, Shatabhisha, Revati (v.21); inner (Madhya) 8 - Mrigashira, Ardra, Uttaraphalguni, Hasta, Mula, Purvashadha, Purvabhadra, Uttarabhadra (v.22), four of them the pillars (v.23): Ardra east, Hasta south, Purvashadha west, Uttarabhadra north.
- Entry (v.24): three stars each from Krittika, Magha, Anuradha and Dhanishta (twelve); the other twelve, outside the pillars, are exit. The test checks that each quarter's trio runs through the three parts of the fort.
- Benefics Moon, Mercury, Venus, Jupiter; malefics Saturn, Sun, Mars, Rahu, Ketu (v.31).
- Rules built (verses 45-60 and 91-93): a rule is listed when the planets standing in the named parts of the fort fit it. The book does not say which outweighs which, so none is chosen: several, even opposed, rules can be listed together. Two places where the Sanskrit and the Hindi differ are marked on the rule (v.48: "veshtaka", the besieger, against the commentary's "defenders").
- Not built: v.66-90 and the later sections, whose conditions are not stated fully enough to test; the turning of the chakra by the first letter of the fort's name (v.25-26) - not needed, as a planet's part of the fort does not depend on it.
- The book's use is a siege or war. Reading a person's horoscope on this chakra (PL9 does) is a modern use; the page offers it and marks it as such. K.K. Joshi's book on it (modern) is not held.

## Nakshatra Sanghatta Chakra (printed pp.148-150, verses 1-15, the diagram at p.150)
- Twenty-seven stars on the edge of a trapezoid of nine rows of triangles: right edge, top to bottom, Ashwini ... Ashlesha; bottom, right to left, Magha ... Jyeshtha; left edge, bottom to top, Mula ... Revati - the zodiac order round the figure (a test).
- Each star is joined by straight lines of the figure to two others (vedha): row k joins the left end to the right end; the slant from each right end meets the bottom point of the same number; the slant from each left end meets the bottom point of the mirrored number. This gives 27 pairs, every star on exactly two. The five pairs the verse itself names (v.2: Ashwini-Revati, Ashwini-Jyeshtha, Magha-Revati, Magha-Ashlesha, Ashlesha-Mula) all come out of this reading, which is its test.
- Rules built: v.6 (a chosen star struck by no planet or only by benefics - no war; struck by a malefic - war), v.7 (Saturn, Mars, Sun or Rahu striking the Moon), v.11 (Sun and Moon on one line), v.12 (Mars and Rahu joined with the Moon at the end of the line), v.13 (Mercury, Venus and Moon in one star: rain and lightning), v.14 (Moon, Sun, Saturn, Rahu in one star: dull weather). Not built: v.4-5 (two kings' name-stars), 8-10, 15 - not stated fully enough.
- A planet in Abhijit is placed in Uttarashadha here, as the chakra has 27 stars. The Rashi Sanghatta chakra that other authors name is not in this chapter.

## Navamsha Ages (PL9 010) - built as "Navamsha counts"
Not in Narapati's Svarodaya (its Amsha chakra, printed pp.96-97, is a name-letter vedha chakra of the 108 padas, not ages). Found elsewhere in the owner's books: the number of navamshas (108 equal divisions of 3d20' from Aries) between the ascendant's navamsha and a planet's, both ends included, read as a year (`src/chart/navamshaCount.js`, `test-navamsha-count.js`, page `/navamsha-years`).
- PL9's number set against each planet is the count from the ascendant's navamsha, ascendant = 1: the PL9 screen of the audit (Aniruddha, 5 Oct 1990) gives Sun 8, Moon 64, Mars 80 and the test reproduces all three from the printed longitudes.
- Prasna Marga (B.V. Raman) ch.10 stanza 50, printed p.140: the count from the Lagna Navamsa to the navamsa of the most evil planet is days, months or years of the querist's life left - a horary use, death only.
- Hora Sara ch.17 vv.88½-91½ as C. Patel (*Navamsa in Astrology* ch.10, pp.100-103) gives it: the count from Saturn's, Rahu's and Mars's navamsha up to the ascendant's, both included, is a year of injury or calamity; from the Moon, Mercury, Jupiter and Venus a year of progeny, wealth and happiness; one navamsha a year. Patel's worked chart is reproduced by the test (Saturn 6, Rahu 52). The Hora Sara e-text we hold (Bhagat's, PDF p.60) words the same place as "add the Navamshas of Shani and Lagna": the two readings differ, the Sanskrit is not held, and the page keeps both without choosing.
- The pasted note's claim that Deva Keralam, BPHS, Jaimini and Bhrigu Nandi Nadi give the rule was not found there (Deva Keralam vol.3 pp.119-122 read: nadiamsa). Brihat Jataka ch.7 stanzas 9-10 (Satyacharya: a planet's years equal the navamshas it has passed over, no more than 12) is a different calculation (navamsha ayurdaya) and is not built.
